    How this estimate was made

    It compares different ways of counting, so treat it as a range, not a lab result.

    1. Chat answers: 0 to 19 sources per answer, so 229 is about 12 times the highest average (19.48) and 75 times the lowest that cites at all (3.05), across four leading assistants with web search on. One study measured 3.05 to 19.48 over 960 answers (Prefer, Sep 2026); another measured 0.07 to 19.08 over 1,000 questions (Slate, Aug 2026). The two disagree on individual assistants, so only the range is used.
    2. Deep-research agents: the best of four averaged 111.21 effective citations per report, and the most precise reached 90.24% citation accuracy, on 100 PhD-level tasks (DeepResearch Bench, 2025). Effective citations count only citations that support a statement.
    3. ProofProbe: 229 unique sources from 60 domains recorded in the research notes of our deepest probe so far (RL Audits, Oct to Nov 2026), with about 600 claims tagged by how well they're supported. It used 10 answers and 22 decisions; a career probe used 40 answers. Two other probes show 17 and 19 sources on their public pages. That's a small sample, and sources recorded aren't scored the same way as effective citations.
    4. Error rates: 45% and 31% come from over 3,000 answers judged by journalists from 22 public broadcasters (EBU and BBC, Oct 2025). 60%+ comes from 1,600 citation queries across eight AI search tools (Tow Center, Mar 2025).

    Sources: Prefer AI answer study · Slate citation study · DeepResearch Bench · EBU and BBC study · Tow Center study
